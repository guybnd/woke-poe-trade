Guy picked option B: over-quality bases keep the look of their row and drop tier, and a mark goes on top so they stand out.

## What changes
- With **Over-quality bases** on, the table now writes its own over-quality rules inside the table, right after Never. There is one per row (build, rank 1-3, unranked) for each drop tier, plus one for no tier. Each takes the table's look for that row and tier, including the rarity text colour and your Magic/Rare looks, then adds the **mark**.
- The mark is a white border by default. **Change mark** on the over-quality card sets the border, sound, beam and map icon (a new `markOnly` mode of `StyleEditor`), and Reset goes back to the default.
  - A mark setting that is switched off adds nothing, so the row keeps its own beam, sound or border.
- Over-quality bases still show where the table hides their drop tier. Never bases with quality stay hidden at any item level.
- NeverSink's `crafting->qualityperfection` over-quality rules are retired while the table owns them, even when every row is set to Off.
- A test drop shown by an over-quality rule opens the mark editor.
- Adds a What's new line `loot-filter-over-quality-look`, with a spot on the card that opens the Gear tab. Also adds a line to release-next-notes.md.
