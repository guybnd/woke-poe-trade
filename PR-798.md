## Why
After #797, Guy's chart still showed a plateau at 1,947 div (476 real). Those reads were saved at Rain Tempter's fake 98 div price. The chart's old clean-up (`priceSpikes`) only removed a glitch that lasted one read, and this one lasted about an hour of reads.

## What changed
- `ledger.ts` `priceGlitches` measures the running price change from the read before a jump: the value change minus what came in, summed over the reads since. A jump of over 25% that stays up and falls back to half or less of its peak within a day counts as a glitch. Each read in between is drawn without its own share of it, so drift during the glitch and a return in steps come out too. Whatever is left at the return counts as a real move from the start.
- The chart does this as it draws, from the saved points. Every user's past spikes heal, and no saved data is changed.
- A tab change (unknown in/out) or more than a day breaks a run, so it is left alone.
- `priceSpikes` keeps its API.
- Tests: Guy's hour of reads, drift, a return in steps, a tab change, and a rise held over a day.

## Review
A separate adversarial review found that the first version subtracted one fixed amount, so drift produced a fake dip. It also skipped the check on the first read in between, and had no time cap. All are fixed in the second commit.

Still open (older than this PR): `diffStash` values a fully removed item at the baseline's price. If a glitched item is sold during a glitch, the drop is booked as a loss instead of a price return. With the price holds from #796 and #797 this should no longer happen.
