## What
Follow-up to #755. The Web got past the thin-jump guard. poe.ninja dropped it for about 8 h, so Net worth's baseline lost its 2.25c price (unpriced lines store 0). It came back at 28,886c, priced only today, and the guard had nothing to compare it to. One user's net worth went from 116k to 318k.

- **Last solid price** (`Baseline.ref`, `ValuedLine.refChaos`): the last poe.ninja price that wasn't your own, wasn't held, and wasn't a one-day price. It's kept through unpriced and held reads. The jump rule now compares against it first.
- **One-day rule** (`holdJump`): a stackable (not gear) whose poe.ninja line has today's price and none before (`pricedOnlyToday`), at 10c or more, counts at its last solid price, or at 0 if it has none, while it's 5× or more over that price. The count stays until poe.ninja prices it on a second day. A baseline from before refs is never trusted for this rule, so the user's poisoned 28,886c is fixed on the next read.
- `heldKind` is saved in the baseline, so a one-day hold that ends gets a fresh 6 h jump hold.

## Backtest (3 days of poe.ninja pulls)
Every card that got a one-day price 5× or more over its earlier price was fake: The Web, The Witch, Rain Tempter, Thunderous Skies, The Blazing Fire, and an Alteration Shard at 214c for 29 h. Real one-day prices (maps, scrying orbs) stayed near their earlier price. In the latest pull, only 2 stackables have this shape: The Web and one map.

## Review
A separate adversarial reviewer found 2 bugs, both fixed in the second commit:
- An empty poe.ninja line (quiet gems and uniques) read as 7 gaps and was zeroed for good.
- A one-day hold that turned into a jump skipped its 6 h.

## Open
